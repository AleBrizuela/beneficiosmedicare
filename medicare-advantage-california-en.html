Medicare Advantage in California: Complete 2026 Guide

If you're a California resident approaching age 65, you've probably heard about Medicare Advantage. It's a very popular option in California, especially because many plans offer $0 premiums, additional benefits like vision, dental and hearing coverage, and broad provider networks.

What is Medicare Advantage (Part C)?

Medicare Advantage, also known as Part C of Medicare, is a private alternative to Original Medicare (Parts A and B). Instead of receiving coverage directly from the federal government, you choose a plan offered by a private insurer such as Kaiser, SCAN, UHC, Humana or Aetna.

The U.S. government (through Medicare) pays these private insurers a fixed rate to provide all your Medicare coverage. In return, many of these plans offer additional benefits that Original Medicare doesn't include, such as vision, dental, hearing and gym memberships.

Who Can Enroll in Medicare Advantage?

To be eligible for Medicare Advantage, you must:

  • Be enrolled in Medicare Part A and Part B
  • Live in the plan's service area (usually your zip code in California)
  • Not have end-stage renal disease (ESRD), unless it is a special D-SNP plan
  • Not have or be receiving services from PACE (Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly)

How Medicare Advantage Works

To understand Medicare Advantage, it's important to know how it works:

Provider Network

Medicare Advantage plans use a provider network. This means the plan has a specific group of doctors, hospitals and other healthcare providers. When you choose Medicare Advantage, you generally must use providers within that network.

There are different types of networks, which we'll explain in more detail later. The important thing to understand is that your choice of doctors and hospitals may be limited to the plan you choose.

Deductibles and Cost Sharing

Medicare Advantage plans have deductibles, copays and coinsurance, as well as annual out-of-pocket spending limits. This is different from Original Medicare, which has no out-of-pocket spending limit.

For example:

  • A plan may have a $0 deductible for doctor visits, but a $15 copay
  • Specialist visits may be $45
  • Prescription drugs may have a copay of $5 to $50 depending on the drug tier
  • Total expenses are typically capped at $5,000 to $8,000 per year

Part D of Medicare (Medications)

Most Medicare Advantage plans in California include prescription drug coverage (Part D). This means you don't need to choose a separate plan for medications. Prescription drug coverage is integrated into your Medicare Advantage plan.

Additional Benefits: Dental, Vision, Hearing, Gym

One reason California residents choose Medicare Advantage is for the additional benefits. Here's what many plans offer:

Dental Benefits

Many Medicare Advantage plans in California include dental benefits, something Original Medicare absolutely does not cover. Typical benefits include:

  • Preventive dental cleanings (typically 2 per year)
  • Dental exams and X-rays
  • Procedures such as fillings and extractions
  • Some plans cover crowns, bridges or partial dentures

Vision Benefits

Vision benefits typically include:

  • Annual eye exams
  • Glasses or contact lenses (one pair of each or every two years)
  • Some plans cover cataract surgery, glaucoma and diabetic retinopathy

Hearing Benefits

Many plans now include hearing coverage:

  • Annual audiograms and hearing evaluations
  • Hearing aids (often up to $2,000 to $3,000 per year)
  • Batteries and repair services

Fitness and Wellness Benefits

Many Medicare Advantage plans offer free gym memberships, fitness centers or wellness programs such as:

  • SilverSneakers membership or similar programs
  • Yoga, water aerobics or group exercise classes
  • Nutrition and wellness programs

Other Common Benefits

Depending on the plan, it may also include:

  • Transportation (rides to medical appointments)
  • Meals after hospitalization
  • Telehealth services
  • Home medical equipment
  • Disease management programs

$0 Premium Plans

One of the most frequently asked questions is, "Are there Medicare Advantage plans with $0 premium?" The answer is yes. Many plans in California offer $0 premium.

What Does $0 Premium Mean?

When a plan has $0 premium, it means you don't pay an additional premium beyond the Medicare Part B premium, which is mandatory. In 2026, the standard Part B premium is approximately $185 per month (subject to change based on income).

$0 premium doesn't mean everything is free. You will still pay:

  • Medicare Part B premium (mandatory)
  • Copays for doctor visits
  • Coinsurance and deductibles as per the plan
  • Prescription drug costs

Why Do They Offer $0 Premium?

Plans offer $0 premium because they receive a capitation (fixed payment) from the federal government for each member. They can offer $0 premium while still making profits by managing care costs efficiently.

Additionally, they offer $0 premium to attract more members and compete with other plans in their area.

Plans for Dual Eligible (D-SNP)

If you are eligible for both Medicare and Medicaid (usually because your income and assets are very low), you may qualify for a D-SNP (Dual-Eligible Special Needs Plan).

What is a D-SNP?

A D-SNP is a special type of Medicare Advantage plan designed specifically for people eligible for both Medicare and Medicaid. These plans integrate Medicare and Medicaid coverage into a single plan.

Benefits of a D-SNP:

  • Coverage of both Medicare and Medicaid in one plan
  • $0 premium (if also eligible for Medicaid savings)
  • Additional Medicaid benefits, such as long-term care
  • Coordinated care services
  • Medication management

Who Qualifies?

You qualify if:

  • You meet Medicare eligibility requirements
  • You are approved for Medicaid in California
  • You live in the plan's service area

Provider Networks: HMO vs PPO

When you choose a Medicare Advantage plan, a key decision is the type of network: HMO or PPO. These affect who your doctor can be and how much you pay.

HMO Plans (Health Maintenance Organization)

An HMO plan requires you to:

  • Choose a primary care physician (PCP) within the network
  • Get referrals from your PCP to see specialists
  • Use doctors and hospitals within the network (except emergencies)
  • See providers within the plan's service area

HMO Advantages:

  • Lower premiums (often $0)
  • Coordinated care between your PCP and specialists
  • Generally predictable costs

HMO Disadvantages:

  • Less flexibility in choosing providers
  • Requires referrals for specialists
  • May be limited if your preferred doctor is not in the network

PPO Plans (Preferred Provider Organization)

A PPO plan allows you to:

  • See specialists without a referral
  • See providers outside the network (at higher cost)
  • Greater overall flexibility in your care

PPO Advantages:

  • Greater flexibility in choosing providers
  • No need for referrals
  • Can see out-of-network providers (although at higher cost)
  • Better if you travel frequently

PPO Disadvantages:

  • Generally higher premiums
  • Higher cost if you use out-of-network providers
  • Less care coordination

How to Compare Medicare Advantage Plans in California

With so many options, how do you compare and choose the best plan? Here's the process:

Plan Comparison Checklist

  1. Verify that your preferred doctors are in the plan's network
  2. Confirm that your prescription medications are covered and at what level (copay)
  3. Compare premiums (even though they may be $0, the total cost can vary)
  4. Review deductibles, copays and out-of-pocket spending limits
  5. Check additional benefits (dental, vision, hearing, gym)
  6. Consider the type of network (HMO vs PPO) and whether it fits your lifestyle
  7. Review plan satisfaction ratings (quality)
  8. Read reviews from current members if possible
  9. Verify whether the plan covers special services you need (specialized vision care, etc.)

Tools to Compare

You can use these tools to compare plans:

  • Medicare.gov Plan Finder: Visit www.medicare.gov and use the Plan Finder to compare plans in your area. You can enter your medications to see specific costs.
  • Direct insurer: Visit the websites of Kaiser, SCAN, UHC, Humana, Aetna and Blue Shield to see specific plans.
  • Medicare agent: A licensed agent like those at Medicare Benefits can compare all available plans in your zip code and help you find the best one for you.

Enrollment Periods and Plan Changes

It's important to understand when you can enroll in or change your Medicare Advantage plan. There are several enrollment periods with different rules.

Initial Enrollment Period (IEP)

When you first become eligible for Medicare (usually the month you turn 65), you have an initial enrollment period of 7 months. During this period, you can:

  • Choose Original Medicare or Medicare Advantage
  • Choose a Medigap plan if you choose Original Medicare
  • Choose a Part D medication plan

This is a "penalty-free" period to change your mind.

Medicare Annual Enrollment Period (AEP)

Each year, from October 15 to December 7, you can change your Medicare plan (either from one Medicare Advantage plan to another, or to Original Medicare).

  • If you change during this period, the new plan starts on January 1 following
  • You can only make one change during this period (you cannot change multiple times)

Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (MA Open Enrollment)

If you are already enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan, you have a special period from January 1 to March 31 to switch to a different Medicare Advantage plan (but cannot switch to Original Medicare).

Qualifying Life Events (Special Changes)

If a specific life event occurs, you can change your plan outside of regular enrollment periods:

  • Move outside your plan's service area
  • Your doctor leaves your plan
  • Your medication is no longer covered
  • Marriage, divorce, birth or adoption of a child
  • Loss of group health coverage (retirement, job loss)
  • Significant plan changes (dramatic cost increase, loss of benefits)

When to Choose Medicare Advantage?

Medicare Advantage is a good option if:

  • Your preferred doctors are in the plan's network
  • You want additional benefits like dental, vision and hearing
  • You prefer low premiums (often $0)
  • You have a limited budget for healthcare costs
  • You don't travel frequently internationally
  • You prefer coordinated care

When to Choose Original Medicare?

Original Medicare is better if:

  • You want maximum flexibility in choosing doctors
  • You have specialist doctors who are not in local Medicare Advantage plans
  • You travel frequently internationally
  • You prefer not to have out-of-pocket spending limits (even though cost is higher)
  • You want to avoid network restrictions

How to Choose the Right Medicare Advantage Plan for You

After understanding your options, here's how to make the right decision:

Step 1: Check Availability in Your Area

Not all plans are available everywhere in California. Check what plans are available in your zip code on Medicare.gov or by calling your insurer.

Step 2: Check Your Doctors

List your preferred doctors (primary care physician, specialists, etc.) and verify which plans include them. This is the most important factor in your decision.

Step 3: Check Your Medications

If you take prescription medications, enter your medications on Medicare.gov and see which plans cover them and at what level (copay). Drug costs can vary significantly between plans.

Step 4: Compare Total Costs

Don't focus only on the $0 premium. Calculate the total cost:

  • Monthly premium
  • Deductible
  • Expected copays for your frequent visits
  • Medication costs

Step 5: Compare Additional Benefits

If vision, dental or hearing are important to you, compare the specific benefits of each plan. Not all plans offer the same additional benefits.

Step 6: Consider the Network

Do you need flexibility (PPO) or are you fine with a closed network (HMO)? If you travel frequently, a PPO may be better. If you have a preferred group of doctors, an HMO with those doctors can work well.
