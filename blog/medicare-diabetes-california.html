Medicare y la Diabetes en California: Que Cubre en 2026?

Publicado el 11 de junio de 2026 • Susana Marcos LLC, Agente de Seguros de Medicare con Licencia • California

La diabetes es una de las condiciones de salud mas comunes entre las personas mayores en California, y manejarla bien cuesta dinero: medidores, tiras, insulina, visitas al doctor. La buena noticia es que Medicare cubre gran parte de ese costo, y en 2026 hay mas proteccion que nunca, sobre todo para el precio de la insulina. Esta guia le explica que cubre Medicare para la diabetes, cuanto paga usted y como aprovechar cada beneficio.

Suministros Para la Diabetes: La Parte B

La Parte B de Medicare cubre los suministros que usa para controlar su azucar en casa. Despues de cumplir el deducible anual de la Parte B, usted normalmente paga el 20% del monto aprobado por Medicare. Entre los suministros cubiertos estan:

  • El medidor de glucosa en la sangre
  • Las tiras reactivas y las lancetas
  • El dispositivo de lancetas y la solucion de control
  • Suministros para la bomba de insulina, cuando es medicamente necesaria

La cantidad de tiras y lancetas que cubre depende de si usa insulina. Quienes usan insulina suelen recibir mas suministros al mes. Para entender que parte de Medicare paga cada cosa, vea nuestra guia de las partes de Medicare.

La Insulina: El Tope de 35 Dolares

Este es el cambio mas importante de los ultimos anos. Por ley federal, su costo de bolsillo por un suministro de un mes de cada insulina cubierta esta limitado a 35 dolares en los planes de la Parte D y en los planes Medicare Advantage con cobertura de medicamentos. Ademas, no hay deducible que pagar primero para la insulina.

Eso significa que aunque su plan tenga un deducible, la insulina se mantiene en 35 dolares al mes desde enero. Si usa una bomba de insulina cubierta por la Parte B, esa insulina tambien tiene el tope de 35 dolares. Si paga mas que eso, vale la pena revisar su plan.

Consejo: Si su insulina le cuesta mas de 35 dolares al mes, quizas esta en el plan equivocado o la farmacia no esta aplicando el beneficio. Un agente con licencia puede revisar su plan de la Parte D sin costo.

Monitores Continuos de Glucosa

Los monitores continuos de glucosa (CGM) han cambiado la vida de muchas personas con diabetes. En lugar de pincharse el dedo varias veces al dia, un sensor mide el azucar de forma automatica. La Parte B cubre los CGM para muchas personas que cumplen los requisitos medicos, por ejemplo quienes usan insulina o han tenido episodios graves de azucar baja. El monitor y los sensores se cubren como equipo medico duradero, y usted paga el 20% despues del deducible.

Educacion, Nutricion y Cuidado de los Pies

Medicare cubre mucho mas que aparatos. Tambien cubre el aprendizaje y la prevencion, que son la clave para evitar complicaciones.

Capacitacion para el autocontrol

Medicare cubre clases de educacion para el autocontrol de la diabetes. Aprende a medir su azucar, ajustar la comida, reconocer las senales de alerta y cuidar sus pies. Muchas veces tiene costo bajo o sin costo.

Terapia de nutricion medica

Si tiene diabetes o enfermedad renal, Medicare cubre sesiones con un dietista o nutricionista registrado para crear un plan de alimentacion. Esta terapia muchas veces se cubre sin costo para usted.

Examenes de los pies y zapatos terapeuticos

La diabetes puede danar los nervios de los pies. Si tiene ese dano, la Parte B cubre un examen de los pies cada seis meses. Tambien puede cubrir zapatos terapeuticos o plantillas especiales para personas con diabetes.

Que Agregan los Planes Medicare Advantage

Muchos planes Medicare Advantage en California incluyen beneficios extra que ayudan a quienes viven con diabetes, como programas de bienestar, transporte a las citas, beneficios para comprar alimentos saludables y, a veces, suministros con menor costo compartido. Estos extras cambian segun el plan y el condado. Medicare and Diabetes in California: What Is Covered in 2026?

Published June 11, 2026 • Susana Marcos LLC, Licensed Medicare Insurance Agent • California

Diabetes is one of the most common health conditions among older adults in California, and managing it well costs money: meters, strips, insulin, doctor visits. The good news is that Medicare covers a large share of that cost, and in 2026 there is more protection than ever, especially for the price of insulin. This guide explains what Medicare covers for diabetes, what you pay, and how to use every benefit.

Diabetes Supplies: Part B

Medicare Part B covers the supplies you use to manage your blood sugar at home. After you meet the annual Part B deductible, you usually pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount. Covered supplies include:

  • Your blood glucose meter
  • Test strips and lancets
  • The lancet device and control solution
  • Insulin pump supplies, when medically necessary

The number of strips and lancets covered depends on whether you use insulin. People who use insulin usually receive more supplies per month. To understand which part of Medicare pays for what, see our guide to the parts of Medicare.

Insulin: The $35 Cap

This is the most important change in recent years. By federal law, your out-of-pocket cost for a one-month supply of each covered insulin is capped at $35 in Part D plans and in Medicare Advantage plans with drug coverage. There is also no deductible to pay first for insulin.

That means even if your plan has a deductible, insulin stays at $35 per month starting in January. If you use an insulin pump covered under Part B, that insulin also has the $35 cap. If you are paying more, it is worth a plan review.

Tip: If your insulin costs more than $35 a month, you may be in the wrong plan or the pharmacy may not be applying the benefit. A licensed agent can review your Part D plan at no cost.

Continuous Glucose Monitors

Continuous glucose monitors (CGMs) have changed life for many people with diabetes. Instead of pricking your finger several times a day, a sensor measures your sugar automatically. Part B covers CGMs for many people who meet the medical requirements, such as those who use insulin or have had serious low blood sugar events. The monitor and sensors are covered as durable medical equipment, and you pay 20% after the deductible.

Education, Nutrition, and Foot Care

Medicare covers much more than devices. It also covers learning and prevention, which are the key to avoiding complications.

Self-management training

Medicare covers diabetes self-management training classes. You learn to check your sugar, adjust your meals, recognize warning signs, and care for your feet. It often has a low cost or no cost.

Medical nutrition therapy

If you have diabetes or kidney disease, Medicare covers sessions with a registered dietitian or nutritionist to build an eating plan. This therapy is often covered at no cost to you.

Foot exams and therapeutic shoes

Diabetes can damage the nerves in your feet. If you have that damage, Part B covers a foot exam every six months. It may also cover therapeutic shoes or special inserts for people with diabetes.

What Medicare Advantage Plans Add

Many Medicare Advantage plans in California include extra benefits that help people living with diabetes, such as wellness programs, transportation to appointments, healthy food allowances, and sometimes lower cost sharing on supplies. These extras vary by plan and county. To compare, see our guide to Medicare Advantage plans in California, and check how they affect your budget on our 2026 Medicare costs page.

Does Medicare cover a continuous glucose monitor?

Yes. Part B covers CGMs for many people with diabetes who meet the medical requirements, such as those who use insulin. They are covered as durable medical equipment.

Does Medicare cover foot exams for people with diabetes?

Yes. With nerve damage in the feet, Part B covers a foot exam every six months, and it may cover therapeutic shoes for people with diabetes.

Between the $35 insulin cap, covered supplies, and free education classes, Medicare gives you real tools to stay healthy without draining your wallet.
